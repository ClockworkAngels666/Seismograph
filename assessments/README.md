# Writing country assessments

The model tells a reader **what** the data says. An assessment adds **so what**: your judgment about where the
country is heading, how confident you are, and what would change your mind. This is the part of the project that
shows you can do the job of a political risk analyst — so it is worth doing well.

## How to add one

1. Open the app's **Assessment builder** page, fill in the sections and click **Download**. You get a file such
   as `NGA.md`. (Or copy `_TEMPLATE.md` and edit it by hand.)
2. On GitHub, open this `assessments` folder → **Add file** → **Upload files** → drop the file → **Commit changes**.
3. Within about ten minutes the assessment appears in the *Analyst assessment* tab of the country profile, and a ✓
   appears in the ranking table.

The file name must be the country's three-letter ISO code (`KEN.md`, `BRA.md`…). Files starting with `_` are ignored.

## What good looks like

**Bottom line up front.** Lead with the conclusion. A reader who stops after three sentences should know the risk
level, the trend and the main driver.

**Key judgments are forecasts, not descriptions.** "Inflation is high" is a description. "Inflation is *likely*
(~70%) to stay above 20% through mid-2027 because…" is a judgment someone can check later.

**Use estimative language consistently.** The builder uses the scale from the US Intelligence Community's
Analytic Standards (ICD 203):

| Term | Probability |
|---|---|
| almost no chance | 1–5% |
| very unlikely | 5–20% |
| unlikely | 20–45% |
| roughly even chance | 45–55% |
| likely | 55–80% |
| very likely | 80–95% |
| almost certain | 95–99% |

**Separate likelihood from confidence.** Likelihood is how probable the event is; confidence is how good your
evidence is. "We assess with *low confidence* that a coup is *unlikely*" is a coherent sentence.

**Scenarios should be distinct and add up to 100%.** Give each a short name, describe what it looks like, and list
signposts — observable events that would tell you that scenario is unfolding.

**Indicators to watch** turn the assessment into a monitoring plan: "Central bank reserves fall below three months
of imports", "The electoral commission delays voter registration".

**Explain disagreements with the model.** The model is backward-looking and relative. If your rating differs, say
why: recent events not yet in the data, or factors the indicators miss. This section is often the most interesting
part for a reader — and for an interviewer.

**Cite sources.** Prefer primary sources (government statements, IMF reports, election commissions) and specialist
outlets (Crisis Group, regional think tanks) over general news.

## Keep a record

Update each assessment every quarter, and keep the old version (for example `archive/NGA_2026-10.md`). After a
year you can compare your judgments with what actually happened — a track record is the strongest evidence of
analytic skill you can show an employer.
